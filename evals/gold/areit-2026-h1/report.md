# AREIT — 1H 2026 (January to June) review

**In one sentence:** In the first half of 2026, AREIT earned ₱1.12 in cash earnings for every ₱1 it declared in dividends for those two quarters, so the dividend is backed by real cash.

**Dividend safety:** green — The dividend is covered by cash earnings.

**Q2 2026 alone (derived):** 1.14x — in April to June by itself, AREIT earned ₱2,998.3M in cash earnings against a ₱2,618.8M Q2 dividend. Worked out by subtracting the Q1 2026 report's figures from the six-month figures, so treat it as a check on the trend, not a reported figure. The derived Q2 net income matches the filing's own April-to-June column.

This review covers six months because the quarterly report (SEC Form 17-Q) only shows cash flows for the year to date.

## Worth knowing
- **Part of the revenue is owed by the sponsor group.** About 2 of every 10 pesos of revenue (24.3%) is interest on malls and hotels leased to companies related to the sponsor, Ayala Land (finance leases), and is often collected later; the filing lists it as a non-cash item. The amount owed under these leases grew ₱20,328.2M, mostly because two malls worth ₱19,476.0M were added under such leases in June.
- **Cash was lent to the sponsor group.** During the six months the REIT lent ₱686.0M to companies in its sponsor's group, equal to 12% of net income.
- **New shares were issued.** The REIT issued 441.1M new shares (11.9% more) on June 25, to Ayala Land and a subsidiary in exchange for Ayala Center Cebu Mall and Ayala Malls Feliz. They were issued at ₱44.15 per share, 20.7% above the ₱36.57 book value (NAV) per share at December 31, 2025, the latest this filing reports before the issue. The filing gives transaction values for the malls, not independent appraisals. The Q2 dividend is shared across these extra shares, while the two malls contributed only a few days of income to this period, so the next quarter is a better test of coverage.
- **More shares are coming, this time priced near book value.** On August 10, after the period, the board approved issuing 462,481,990 new shares to Ayala Land and four subsidiaries for commercial properties with a transaction value of ₱17,333.8M, which the filing says was "validated by a third-party fairness opinion" (p.21). That works out to ₱37.48 per share (our calculation from the two disclosed figures; the filing states no price), 0.3% below the ₱37.60 book value per share, unlike the earlier swaps at ₱41.50 and ₱44.15.
- **A large cash purchase was approved after the period.** The board also approved buying Fairmont Raffles Hotel Makati condominium units for ₱2,616.9M in cash (p.21), about 84.8 times the ₱30.9M in cash the REIT held at June 30. It had ₱23.5 billion of unused bank credit lines (p.21), so the purchase will likely be funded mostly with borrowing.
