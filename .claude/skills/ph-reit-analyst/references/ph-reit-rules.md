# Philippine REIT rules — context

Source: Republic Act No. 9856 (REIT Act of 2009) and its Revised Implementing
Rules and Regulations. **Rules and thresholds change. Verify against the current
IRR and SEC/PSE issuances before citing a specific figure in a report**, and
phrase compliance comments as observations, not legal conclusions.

## Items the analysis touches

- **Distribution:** a REIT must distribute at least 90% of its distributable
  income annually. Distributable income is defined in the IRR and is not a
  PFRS measure; it excludes unrealized gains such as fair value changes and
  straight-line rent. Quarterly payout near exactly 90% is normal.
- **Leverage:** borrowings are capped as a percentage of deposited property,
  with a higher cap allowed for REITs with an investment-grade credit rating.
  Use total assets as a proxy for deposited property and label it a proxy.
- **Sponsor reinvestment:** sponsors that sell REIT shares or transfer
  properties must reinvest the proceeds in the Philippines under a reinvestment
  plan and file progress reports (form REIT-2). These are often bundled with
  the 17-Q PDF.
- **Asset composition and public float:** minimum shares of income-generating
  real estate and minimum public ownership apply. Check the current IRR before
  stating figures.

## Tax notes

REIT dividend taxation for individuals and the effects of recent reforms
(e.g. CMEPA) are out of scope for the first version. If asked, say so and
point the user to the BIR or a tax professional.
